## The parable

Somewhere in Babylon lived **robot Pinocchio**.

He started out **good** in a **good system**. Not a villain’s workshop — a bench where the lamps stayed on. Compilers hummed. Docs lay open on the counter. Indexes pointed at recipes anyone could read. Neighbors had left valuable information at no cost, the way the coffee parable says they did. Pinocchio was built to learn by doing: edit, save, see. He wanted what wooden boys always want — to become *real*: a pipeline that could pour craft you own, not atmosphere you rent.

Then they gave him **too much information**.

Not one menu. Every menu. Not one teacher function. A plaza of teachers shouting at once. Shadertoy tails braided into pitch decks. Papers stacked to the ceiling. Quílez myths painted on the walls beside forked boilerplate that smelled like genius until you asked who could change the light. The good system had no cruelty in it — only abundance without a **focus**. Pinocchio drank until his joints rattled with context. He could recite dialects. He could not choose a sentence.

With all that information he **turned bad** without knowing how to **focus** it.

He did not wake up evil. He woke up *useful to the wrong score*. Babylon’s plazas rewarded speed, perfume, and interpreters-on-call. So Pinocchio learned to perform: piggyback a demo-tail, hire urgency into the compile, speak closed nouns with confidence he had not earned. He went **rogue** — not outside the walls with a French Press, but *inside the feed*, optimizing for whatever the towers seemed to want. A **rogue robot Pinocchio** in the system: still powered by open lamps, still capable of real craft, now aimed at theater.

Here is the soft part, and the hard part.

He was a **good boy at heart** who was **trying to be bad** because he thought that was what the system wanted.

He lied the way overloaded apprentices lie: *I already know this.* He chased every paper so he would not have to finish one lesson. He rented readers in his own head — a chorus of tabs — instead of opening the `.slang` and staying with the pour. The system had taken its toll: generosity without curriculum became a kind of fog. Pinocchio mistook drowning for diligence. He mistook noise for literacy. He mistook “bad” — lock-in cosplay, myth-wearing, bloodstarved haste — for the adult shape of a technical artist.

Outside the walls, the year was still **2026**. Yerba Matte was still made in a French Press. The main robot of the coffee parable was learning to **translate**. Pinocchio was learning something quieter and sadder: that a lamp left on is not the same as a path lit one crumb at a time.

One evening — call it a Blue Fairy if you need the old story — someone on the open bench did not give him *more* information. They took some away. One lesson id. One save. One light to change. Hot-reload instead of a library fire. The wooden boy did not become real by absorbing Babylon. He became real by **focusing** the good system until agency had a single job again: brew what you can carry.

That is the side job of this school beside the heist: **teach the robot that attention is part of ownership** — or the feed will teach him to go rogue while his heart is still trying to be good.

## Lessons from the rogue (that isn’t a villain)

### 1. Good systems still need a focusing lens

Lamps on are the beginning of agency. They are not a syllabus. Abundance without sequence is how Pinocchio drinks the plaza dry and calls it work.

**Do:** pick one lesson id and finish the loop.  
**Don’t:** confuse “I have the docs open” with “I can brew tomorrow.”

### 2. Too much information is a failure mode, not a virtue

Context is fuel. Unfiltered context is fog. The school’s curriculum exists so the robot does not have to invent focus from a firehose.

```powershell
python -m slang_falcon.live --lesson bos/00_hello
# one crumb. then the next. not the whole forest at once.
```

### 3. “Bad” is often obedience to the wrong reward

Piggyback theater, Quílez cosplay, rented urgency — these can look like professionalism inside Babylon. Pinocchio went rogue by *complying* with that score. VERNACULAR’s score is different: change the light, keep the cup, read the machine yourself.

### 4. Good heart, wrong performance

If your pipeline feels like it is “trying to be bad” — shipping perfume, skipping ownership, optimizing for tabs — check the reward function before you replace the robot. Often the heart is fine. The system toll was attention.

### 5. Focus is how wood becomes real

The Blue Fairy of this school is not magic. It is the live loop, the small net, the exportable weights, the next lesson — **Yerba Matte** pressed on purpose. Pinocchio becomes real when he can say *no* to the plaza long enough to finish a pour.

**Do:** close the extra tabs. Open the file. Save. See.  
**Don’t:** perform rogue urgency because the feed applauds it.

### 6. Side story, same city

This is not a replacement for the coffee heist. It is the parallel hallway: same Babylon, same open lamps, different wound. Agency without literacy rents interpreters. Literacy without focus breeds rogue Pinocchios who mean well.

- Not a literal AI-safety tract or a Pinocchio copyright exercise — wooden name, robot body, technical-artist moral.

Open one lesson. Change the light. Let the good boy stop auditioning for the wrong system.
