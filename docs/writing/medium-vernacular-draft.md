## Draft

Once there were two wanderers who got lost the way technical artists always get lost: not by walking too far, but by standing still inside someone else’s frame.

The forest was beautiful. That was the problem. Every clearing offered a postage-stamp playground — a glowing rectangle the size of a business card, spinning a sphere or a raymarched blob, captioned *look what we can do*. The paths between stamps were paved with **boilerplate**: forked tails of other people’s `mainImage`, Shadertoy perfume sprayed on a pitch deck, node jewelry that sang until you asked who owned the runtime. Deep in the trees hung a plaza rumor called the **Quílez myth** — the story that everyone already works like pure math from the hip, SDFs blooming from innate genius. Most towers in that forest did not. They **piggybacked**. When the perfume thinned, they hired **bloodstarved codeheads** — underpaid, over-urgent readers — to keep the illusion compiling before Monday.

The wanderers had names that mattered less than their hunger. Call them Hansel and Gretel if you like fairytales. Call them a craftsperson and their pipeline if you prefer 2026. What they shared was a refusal: they would not rent literacy forever. They would leave **breadcrumbs** — not crumbs of bread that birds eat, but crumbs of **code**: small, hot, owned. A school that spoke in the open stack’s tongue. They called the path **VERNACULAR**, because the job was never to storm Babylon’s gates. The job was to **translate** — coffee into Yerba Matte, closed menu into French Press, rented reader into hands that can change the light.

They walked.

### First crumb — the live lamp

The first crumb was a lamp that stayed on when they saved a file.

Not a museum of old GLSL tricks behind glass. Not a gated product tour. A **live Slang desk**: edit, compile, see. Lessons in a curriculum you can step through with your thumbs — Book of Shaders steam rising into playground craft, then into the strange modern weather of autodiff and tiny nets. On Windows the window even wobbled like an old desktop spirit had opinions; on every platform the point was the same: **save → image**. The forest’s postage stamps wanted you to admire. The lamp wanted you to **own the loop**.

> *Agency starts when the lamps stay on — compilers, docs, open weights — and you read the machine yourself.*

### Second crumb — color as material

Farther in, the forest tried a softer trap: color as decoration. Pickers as toys. Gradients as wallpaper for a slide.

The wanderers left a different crumb. **Color as material** — something you tune the way a sound engineer rides a fader, not something you screenshot for mood. In a live school, a hue is not a costume change; it is a parameter with consequences. Light folds. Surfaces remember. The point of a picker next to a lesson is not chrome for chrome’s sake. It is to teach that technical artistry is *hands in the pour*, not applause for someone else’s bake.

They did not rant long. They opened the `.slang` and changed the light.

### Third crumb — the postage stamp and the island beyond it

The forest loved small frames. Small frames keep you polite. Small frames keep your look inside someone else’s export story — Houdini / Notch class craft as *example*, useful, high-end, and still a cup that stays in the city.

So the wanderers left a crumb that pointed past the stamp: an **abstract studio** waiting in the next clearing — Falcor as a wider stage. Picture it the way a fairytale pictures a cottage before you reach the door: a **cube on an island**, ocean and sky holding the horizon, Julia and Mandelbrot whispering in the folds of the water, a **Gaussian network** breathing like memory in the mist. Not AAA cosplay. A lesson world — walkable beacons, a weird Shader Man built of UV boxes, stations that explain *why this light is a lesson*. Round 1 is still the live 2D desk that ships today; the island is the path they are cutting. Breadcrumbs are honest when they admit which crumbs are warm in the hand and which are still cooling on the trail.

> *Shaders should live in an environment — not only in postage-stamp playgrounds that teach admiration without agency.*

### Fourth crumb — folds (memory, not magic)

In fairytales, the forest resets every night. In graphics, people call that a bug or a feature depending on the invoice.

The wanderers left crumbs of **fold and feedback**: trails, ping-pong, the idea that a cup can remember the last sip. Not mysticism. Memory. The forest sells atmosphere by the hour; a fold is how your own bench keeps time. When a lesson says *ping-pong*, it is teaching the robot — your pipeline — that autonomy is a loop with state, not a vibes prompt.

### Fifth crumb — the teacher and the tiny mind

Deep enough into the trees, the Quílez myth got loud again: *think harder and you are already that person*. The wanderers answered with a quieter crumb — **name a teacher**. A Disney BRDF that knows how light should behave. Train a small MLP that remembers the teacher the way a barista remembers a regular. Export weights you can carry. Infer in-shader. Compare teacher | network | abs-diff like a strip of evidence, not a miracle.

This is modern rendering as practiced now: Khronos Slang, autodiff in the shader, neural shading that does not trap you behind a host you cannot redistribute. The myth wanted innate genius. The crumb wanted **literacy you can bottle**.

> *Quílez is a craft exemplar, not a staffing model. Exemplars are for learning from — not for cosplay while someone else reads the file.*

### Sixth crumb — refuse the rented tongue

Near the plaza, strangers still begged for interpreters. The machines were literate; the crowd rented literacy by the minute. Babylon’s towers still poured what they called coffee. Outside the walls, the year was already speaking **Yerba Matte** — same craft, open nouns, deed of title clear.

The wanderers’ last crumbs were not features. They were **refusals**, soft and sharp:

- Closed ecosystems are fine as craft references. They are not the only address your work may live at.
- Piggyback demo-tails are not a school.
- Hiring desperate readers to prop up a genius story is not a pipeline — it is a caste the school refuses to expand.
- You should leave able to **compile and sell** (or give away) what you make — stream it, syndicate it, embed it — without renting someone else’s sandbox forever.

VERNACULAR’s tone, if it has one, is short on sermon and long on runnable labs. Compare commercial tools when the trade-off clarifies. Then open the file.

### The cottage (moral)

Hansel and Gretel’s story ends with a house of candy and a witch. Ours ends with something less cinematic and more useful: a **map you keep**.

The dark forest was never the math. The math was always free enough if you dared read it — compilers humming, docs on the counter, papers lit like streetlamps. The hard part was teaching yourself not to rent the tongue. Technical artists keep the map. Vernacular craft over mythic genius. French Press over house cup. Breadcrumbs of Slang over stolen coffee theater.

If you meet the wanderers on the path, they will not sell you innate brilliance. They will hand you a crumb that compiles.

Change the light. Let the Yerba Matte walk.
